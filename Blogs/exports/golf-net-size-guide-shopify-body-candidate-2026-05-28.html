If you are trying to choose a golf practice net, start with your space, not the product photo.

For many home golfers, a 7' x 7' net is the right starting point for a garage, basement, shared room, or compact backyard setup. If you want more confidence with driver, faster swings, family use, or a simulator-style build, move up to an 8' wide Pro setup, a larger Pro Series net, or a simulator bay. If your setup is indoors or near walls, windows, cars, people, or anything expensive, side barriers should be part of the plan.

The goal is simple: choose the smallest setup that still lets you make a normal swing without feeling like you are protecting the room.

Why Golf Net Size Matters

A golf net is not just a frame and some mesh. In a real home setup, it becomes the outline of your safe practice zone.

If that outline is too small, your practice changes. You avoid driver. You slow down. You shorten your swing. You notice the ceiling, the wall, the car, the window, or the light fixture behind the net. Instead of working on your game, you start protecting the room.

The right size does the opposite. It gives you enough net around the target that a bad swing still feels contained. That is what makes home practice useful: you can focus on tempo, contact, start line, and repetition instead of worrying about where the ball might go.

Step 1: Measure Width, Height, And Depth

Before comparing nets, write down three numbers.

Width is the clear space from wall to wall, fence to fence, or object to object. Do not measure the room as if it were empty if shelves, bikes, doors, or storage racks will still be there.

Height is the clear ceiling height from the floor to the lowest obstruction. In a garage, check door rails, open garage doors, lights, beams, pipes, and storage racks.

Depth is the distance from the back wall to the farthest point where you can stand and swing comfortably.

Once you have those numbers, think of your setup as a box. The net, the ball, the hitting mat, and the golfer all have to fit inside that box.

As a practical starting point, most home setups need:

  • About 1 foot between the back of the net and the wall so the net can move and absorb the ball.
  • Around 3 to 4 feet of net depth, depending on the frame.
  • Around 8 to 10 feet from the ball to the net.
  • Around 2 to 3 feet behind the golfer for backswing and finish space.

If those numbers make your space feel too tight, choose a smaller net, move the hitting position, or rethink the room before buying.

Step 3: Decide Whether You Need Side Barriers

Side barriers are not about ego. They are about environment.

If you are outdoors with plenty of open space and you are an experienced golfer, you may not need them. If you are indoors, sharing the setup with family, letting guests hit, practicing near windows, or hitting beside cars and storage, Side Barriers are a smart safety upgrade.

Use side barriers when:

  • The net is near a wall, window, vehicle, or person.
  • Kids, guests, or new golfers will use the setup.
  • Your miss can be a slice, hook, pull, or block.
  • You want the setup to feel relaxed instead of stressful.

This is also where a package can make sense. If you know you need a net, turf, side barriers, sandbags, and tees, compare the Home Package and Pro Package before assembling everything piece by piece.

Golf Net Size Checklist Before You Buy

Before choosing a net, answer these questions:

  • What is the clear width of the space?
  • What is the lowest ceiling or obstruction height?
  • How much depth do you have from wall to hitting position?
  • Will you hit driver, or mostly wedges and irons?
  • Is your miss pattern small, or can it move far left/right?
  • Will kids, guests, or multiple people use the setup?
  • Is the net near walls, windows, cars, furniture, or people?
  • Do you need turf, side barriers, sandbags, and tees?
  • Are you building a simulator now or later?

If you are still between two sizes, choose the setup that lets you make your normal swing with less hesitation.

FAQ

What size golf net do I need for a garage?

Many garage golfers start with a 7' x 7' net if space is tight. If you have more width, want to hit driver regularly, or want a more confident target window, compare Pro and larger Pro Series options.

Is a 7' x 7' golf net big enough for driver?

It can be, depending on your room, swing speed, ball position, and miss pattern. If you hit driver often or your miss can move far offline, a wider Pro setup or side barriers may be a better fit.

How much ceiling height do I need for indoor golf practice?

The net is only part of the ceiling-height question. You also need enough room to swing the club safely. Check your full swing with the longest club you plan to use, and watch for garage rails, beams, lights, and open doors.

How far should a golf net be from the wall?

Leave space behind the net so it can absorb the ball. A practical starting point is about 1 foot of clearance, but final setup depends on the product, ball speed, and room layout.

Do I need side barriers?

Side barriers are recommended when the net is indoors, near walls or windows, used by guests or kids, or placed anywhere an offline shot could create a safety problem.

Should I buy a net or a simulator bay?

Choose a net if your main goal is simple home practice and ball return. Choose a simulator bay if you are building around a screen, projector, launch monitor, and dedicated simulator experience.
